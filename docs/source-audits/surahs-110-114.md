Researched 2026-10-23. These five lessons contain **23 ayahs and 100 word occurrences**. All Urdu word glosses, translations, grammatical explanations, dictionary summaries and concise tafsir notes are original AI-assisted editorial drafts. They are **not scholar-reviewed** and are not attributed to a named Urdu translator. No third-party Urdu book or dictionary was imported.

## Arabic inventory and method

The independent fixture `inventory-110-114.json` was recorded from the visible Uthmani verse text on [An-Nasr](https://quran.com/an-nasr), [Al-Masad](https://quran.com/al-masad), [Al-Ikhlas](https://quran.com/al-ikhlas), [Al-Falaq](https://quran.com/al-falaq), and [An-Nas](https://quran.com/an-nas). Verse-number glyphs and the standalone pause marker after 110:3:4 were excluded from word tokens; the observed word spelling, including Uthmani marks and tatweel carrying dagger alif, was retained. Basmalah is not assigned a numbered occurrence in these five surahs.

The Corpus word-by-word pages independently confirm the order, boundaries and 19/23/15/23/20 counts. Its text-browser representation substitutes images for Arabic word glyphs: this fixture is therefore accurately described as Quran.com text cross-checked against Corpus occurrence inventories, **not a raw Corpus Arabic export**. Direct Quran.com API word retrieval returned HTTP 403, so it was not used as evidence. Segments are educational morphological units; marks indicating recitation or vocalisation are not independently meaningful lexical letters.

## Analysis decisions and cautions

- **110:1–3:** `إِذَا` opens a temporal condition answered by `فَسَبِّحْ`. `يَدْخُلُونَ` is a circumstantial clause for the people; `أَفْوَاجًا` is a further circumstantial accusative of its subject. The imperative pair is Form II (تفعیل) and Form X (استفعال). `تَوَّابًا` is an intensive nominal predicate of كان, not a finite verb.
- **111:1:** `يَدَا` is nominative dual with its nun removed for construct state; the feminine `تَبَّتْ` has a feminine marker, not an attached subject pronoun. `أَبِي` is genitive with ya under the five-noun inflection.
- **111:2:2:** `أَغْنَىٰ / يُغْنِي` is **Form IV, إفعال**. The individual Corpus page omits the numbered form, and its dictionary groups some perfect occurrences under I. This is not propagated. The independent [Cairo Academy غني entry](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/search_engine/roots/غني) distinguishes غَنِيَ from أَغْنَى and explicitly supplies يُغْنِي and the sufficiency/benefit sense. The form determination follows that paradigm and its أَفْعَلَ / يُفْعِلُ pattern; a Corpus discussion comment is not treated as authoritative evidence.
- **111:2:5:** the Corpus morphology labels `مَا` relative, while its Arabic i‘rab chooses a masdariyya construction, equivalent to his earning. Both are disclosed; the relative analysis is the primary learner entry. The first `مَا` remains negative in the chosen analysis. Al-‘Ukbari also permits interrogative rhetoric for the first occurrence; it is not the primary lesson reading.
- **111:3:1:** `سَيَصْلَىٰ` is an **active** Form I imperfect of صَلِيَ / يَصْلَى, with a future prefix. English passive-sounding glosses do not make the Arabic passive.
- **111:4:** the Hafs token is **حَمَّالَةَ**, accusative. The selected analysis is accusative censure with an understood أَذُمُّ. `وَامْرَأَتُهُ` is linked to the subject of يَصْلَى in the selected analysis. The independent [Al-‘Ukbari text](https://quranpedia.net/surah/1/111/book/309) preserves other constructions and readings; the lesson acknowledges the separate-subject alternative without changing the stored Hafs spelling.
- **111:3:3:** ذَات is the feminine relational noun meaning possessing; no unsupported lexical root is invented. The divine proper name الله follows Al-Fatihah’s **editorial lexical grouping under أ ل ه**, with the exact same root-meaning and caveat text. Corpus tags it as a proper noun and does not supply that root assignment: this grouping is not claimed as a Corpus tag or a settled etymology. The lexical grouping keeps root learning consistent across chapters; all four name occurrences explicitly preserve the etymological caveat.
- **112:1:** the primary construction takes هُوَ as the pronoun of the affair, with الله أحد a subject/predicate clause. [Al-‘Ukbari](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/69/494/index.php) records other valid analyses; this lesson labels its selection rather than claiming unanimity. The same classical passage discusses alternative etymologies for أحد; the data follows the Corpus indexing أ ح د.
- **112:3–4:** `يَلِدْ` is active jussive; `يُولَدْ` is passive jussive; both follow لَمْ. `يَكُن` is the jussive defective verb, with deleted medial waw, أَحَدٌ its delayed subject and كُفُوًا the advanced predicate in the selected analysis. لَهُ attaches to that predicate. The root of كُفُو is ك ف أ, despite its surface spelling.
- **113:** مَا is relative in مِنْ شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ. Temporal إِذَا does not require inventing an unexpressed separate conditional answer here. غَاسِق and حَاسِد are active participles; النَّفَّاثَات is an intensive feminine plural noun, not a finite Form II verb. The dictionary [حسد](https://www.arabicacademy.gov.eg/ar/search_engine/roots/حسد) explicitly records both imperfect vowel patterns; the lesson shows يَحْسُدُ / يَحْسِدُ rather than inferring one uniquely from the perfect حَسَدَ.
- **114:** مَلِكِ and إِلٰهِ continue the preceding genitive through the chosen substitution analysis. الخَنَّاس is an adjective. وَسْوَاس and يُوَسْوِسُ have **four root consonants و س و س**; the verb is **quadriliteral Form I, فَعْلَلَ / يُفَعْلِلُ**, never triliteral Form I or تفعیل. مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ is presented as identifying the whisperer's kind, following the Corpus circumstantial analysis.
- `النَّاس` follows the Corpus root index ن و س, without presenting the root-index choice as a settled historical etymology or translating every occurrence as the meaning of a hypothetical base verb.
