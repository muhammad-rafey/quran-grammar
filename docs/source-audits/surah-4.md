## Sources and method

- **Morphology:** Quranic Arabic Corpus morphology 0.4 (Kais Dukes, GNU GPL with the Corpus terms of use), a verbatim distribution checked by SHA-256 `910b721a8f04f4334ac5d4c62b19e858f5d720c525bb975f2e750ea2c6bb532d`. It was consulted locally for segmentation, part of speech, root, lemma, verb form, voice, mood and case; it is not bundled or redistributed.
- **Word types and roots** are derived deterministically from the Corpus stem tags with the mapping that reproduces root assignments for all 230 word occurrences of the existing lessons, including words with no root. A preposition with an attached pronoun (لَهُ، بِهِ، عَلَيْهِمْ) is a particle, following Surahs 1, 105 and 112. Words without a Corpus root, such as proper names, keep no root.
- **Syntax:** the Corpus word-by-word pages (English morphology and Arabic i‘rab for every word) and verse grammar pages (Arabic i‘rab notes adapted from the Quran Printing Complex), retrieved 2026-11-13. 12 of 176 verse pages carry no grammar note: 5, 8, 52, 76, 99, 100, 106, 132, 136, 138, 158, 167.
- **Verb paradigms** (باب، ماضی، مضارع، وزن) for all 928 verb occurrences come from a shared library of 657 verb lexemes for this expansion edition. Form I vowel classes were decided from imperfects attested across the whole Corpus and standard lexica (Lane, Lisan al-Arab, al-Mu‘jam al-Wasit). The edition’s library review and subsequent corrections are recorded in its handoff and adjudication reports.

Translations consulted for meaning only: Saheeh International and Mufti Taqi Usmani. The Urdu wording is original and concise; it is not a translation of these works.

## Editorial checks

The lesson is organized into 113 chunks of whole ayahs. No review-result snapshot was supplied. This source audit therefore does not certify completion of independent reviews, fixes or final confirmations. The intended workflow requires independent grammar and meaning/tafsir reviews and a clean final confirmation after every correction, including minor findings.
The chunk validator checks the exact Arabic word, segments that rejoin to the exact word, ids and order, rule references, verb-note presence, phrase coverage of every word, and the absence of URLs or Latin text. AI review and automated checks are not scholarly review.

## Corrections to Corpus tags

These occurrences carry an adjudicated correction or a documented source/analysis disagreement. The lessons follow the recorded contextual analysis; the raw Corpus data itself is unchanged.

- **4:3:28** تَعُولُوا۟: QAC tags تَعُولُوا۟ jussive, but أَلَّا is أَنْ (masdariyya, nasb particle) + negative لَا; the dropped nun is the sign of nasb, and the ayah note itself treats «ألا تعولوا» as an interpreted masdar: subjunctive.
- **4:12:22** يُوصِينَ: QAC gives يُوصِينَ the pseudo-lemma yuwSiy as Form I; the active prefix vowel u with kasra before the final radical is the Form IV imperfect of أَوْصَى (as يُوصِيكُمُ 4:11 and يُوصِى 4:11). Active, mabni on sukun with the feminine nun as subject.
- **4:42:8** تُسَوَّىٰ: QAC gives تُسَوَّىٰ the pseudo-lemma tusaw~aY` as Form I; the doubled waw (shadda) makes it the Form II passive of سَوَّى (Form I passive would be تُسْوَى). Indicative with implied damma after لَوْ, ٱلْأَرْضُ its na'ib al-fa'il.
- **4:43:33** لَـٰمَسْتُمُ: QAC tags لَـٰمَسْتُمُ as Form I; the vocalised Hafs word belongs to لَامَسَ (Form III), as decided and double-reviewed in the edition-2 verb-paradigm library entry (lamasu|I|ACT). The lexeme handle is kept; follow the reviewed paradigm.
- **4:49:13** يُظْلَمُونَ: QAC tags يُظْلَمُونَ jussive (and the generated i'rab line repeats مجزوم), but the retained nun of the five verbs shows it is indicative; لَا here is plain negation of a third-person passive statement, not prohibition (which would drop the nun). The verse note coordinates وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ as a statement with بَلِ ٱللَّهُ يُزَكِّى.
- **4:61:4** تَعَالَوْا۟: QAC tags تَعَالَوْا۟ as Form IV; the vocalised Hafs word belongs to تَعَالَى (Form VI), as decided and double-reviewed in the edition-2 verb-paradigm library entry (taEaAla|IV|ACT). The lexeme handle is kept; follow the reviewed paradigm.
- **4:64:6** لِيُطَاعَ: QAC tags لِيُطَاعَ as Form I; the vocalised Hafs word belongs to أُطِيعَ (Form IV), as decided and double-reviewed in the edition-2 verb-paradigm library entry (yuTaAEu|I|PASS). The lexeme handle is kept; follow the reviewed paradigm.
- **4:77:47** تُظْلَمُونَ: QAC tags تُظْلَمُونَ jussive (and the generated i'rab line repeats مجزوم), but the retained nun shows it is indicative; وَلَا is negation in a promise (injustice will not be done to you), not prohibition, which would read تُظْلَمُوا۟. The verse note coordinates it with the nominal sentence وَٱلْـَٔاخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ.
- **4:103:11** ٱطْمَأْنَنتُمْ: QAC tags ٱطْمَأْنَنتُمْ as Form XII; the vocalised Hafs word belongs to اِطْمَأَنَّ (Form QIV), as decided and double-reviewed in the edition-2 verb-paradigm library entry ({Toma>an~a|XII|ACT). The lexeme handle is kept; follow the reviewed paradigm.
- **4:105:10** أَرَىٰكَ: QAC tags أَرَىٰكَ a 1st-person singular subjunctive imperfect, but in بِمَآ أَرَىٰكَ ٱللَّهُ the nominative ٱللَّهُ is its subject and no particle of nasb precedes it: it is the Form IV perfect أَرَى (He showed you), 3rd masculine singular, in the relative clause of مَا. The generated i'rab line 'مضارع منصوب' follows the faulty tag.
- **4:108:14** يَرْضَىٰ: QAC tags يَرْضَىٰ subjunctive, but in مَا لَا يَرْضَىٰ the لَا is plain negation inside a relative clause and nothing governs nasb; it is indicative with an implied damma on the alif maqsura.
- **4:115:6** مَا: QAC labels mā relative. The verse i‘rab explicitly treats it as a masdar particle. The interpreted masdar is the genitive complement of the preceding adverb.
- **4:124:15** يُظْلَمُونَ: QAC tags يُظْلَمُونَ jussive, but its nun is retained (sign of raf') after negative لَا; it is joined to the nominal answer فَأُو۟لَـٰٓئِكَ يَدْخُلُونَ, which is not in jazm, so it is indicative.
- **4:127:9** يُتْلَىٰ: QAC tags يُتْلَىٰ subjunctive, but in وَمَا يُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْكُمْ the مَا is relative and no particle of nasb precedes; it is an indicative passive with an implied damma on the alif maqsura.
- **4:140:11** يُكْفَرُ: QAC omits PASS, but يُكْفَرُ has prefix-u with fatha on the fa (active Form I would be يَكْفُرُ): it is the Form I passive (they are disbelieved in), with بِهَا as na'ib al-fa'il, parallel to the passive وَيُسْتَهْزَأُ بِهَا.
- **4:171:4** تَغْلُوا۟: QAC 0.4 tags لَا تَغْلُوا۟ 2nd person dual (the generated i'rab calls the suffix الألف), but it addresses يَـٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَـٰبِ (cf. دِينِكُمْ) and the Uthmani unpronounced ا۟ is alif al-fariqa after wāw al-jamā'a: 2MP jussive after prohibitive لَا, nun deleted, the wāw is the subject.

## Analysis decisions and cautions

Notes recorded by the authors and reviewers for scholarly review; they mark alternative analyses, source disagreements and editorial choices.

- **4:1:1:** QAC tags the stem أَيُّهَا as a nominative noun (and folds the tanbīh hā into the stem). Written per the vocative convention and the nominative rule note: أَيُّ is a singular definite munādā built on ḍamma in maḥall naṣb; هَا is the particle of tanbīh. Not tagged nominative.
- **4:1:2:** QAC iʿrab gives only «اسم مرفوع». Written as badal of أَيُّ, nominative following its form; ʿaṭf bayān or naʿt are the usual alternatives and are mentioned briefly. QAC marks it MP; it is treated as an ism jamʿ (collective) and not as a broken plural, per the broken-plural rule.
- **4:1:5:** QAC iʿrab gives only «اسم موصول». Analysed as naʿt of رَبَّكُمُ in maḥall naṣb, parallel to the ayah grammar note that calls the second ٱلَّذِى naʿt of the divine name.
- **4:1:9:** Lemma kept as the feminine وَاحِدَة to match the QAC lemma; the masculine base is وَاحِد. Tagged number-words for the one/two agreement rule; the iʿrab role is ṣifa.
- **4:1:15:** Lemma given as the singular رَجُل per the guide's singular-base convention; the QAC lemma is the plural رِجَال.
- **4:1:16:** كَثِيرًۭا is analysed as ṣifa of رِجَالًۭا (QAC صفة منصوبة). Muyassar glosses ونساء كثيرات, so the learner text says the sense of abundance also applies to the women; there is no separate adjective in the text.
- **4:1:21:** Ḥafṣ تَسَآءَلُونَ (light sīn) is the Form VI imperfect تَتَسَاءَلُونَ with one tāʾ dropped. Other canonical readings assimilate the tāʾ (تَسَّاءَلُونَ). The packet does not state this; it is standard qirāʾāt/ṣarf knowledge. The assimilation tag rests on it.
- **4:1:23:** Follows the QAC grammar note: وَٱلْأَرْحَامَ is accusative, coordinated with the divine name (i.e. fear [breaking] the ties of the womb), as in Muyassar, Saʿdi and Ibn Kathir (Ibn ʿAbbās and others). Ibn Kathir also reports Ibrāhīm, Mujāhid and al-Ḥasan on the oath «I ask you by Allah and by the womb». That sense matches the genitive reading of Ḥamza (وَٱلْأَرْحَامِ, coordinated with the pronoun of بِهِۦ). Some grammarians also explain the Ḥafṣ accusative as coordinated with the maḥall of بِهِۦ. Neither alternative is stated in the learner text; a scholar should check whether one should be mentioned.
- **4:1:26:** كَانَ is explained as denoting continuity with a divine attribute (consistent with the ك و ن root note), and its ism is the hidden هُوَ.
- **4:1:27:** QAC iʿrab gives only «جار ومجرور». The attachment to رَقِيبًۭا and its fronting for emphasis are the standard analysis, written without an explicit packet source; tagged fronting.
- **4:1:28:** QAC tags رَقِيبًۭا as a noun (N); the app keeps pos noun. Morphology calls it an intensive faʿīl from the transitive رَقَبَ (rule intensive); some grammarians would call it ṣifa mushabbaha.
- **4:1:** The tafsir summary identifies the single soul as Ādam and the mate as Ḥawwāʾ (Muyassar, Ibn Kathir). It omits the rib hadith and the view of al-Ḍaḥḥāk (Allah «whom you invoke in transactions and contracts»), a minor variant reported by Ibn Kathir.
- **4:2:4:** QAC tags this wa as REM and the i‘rab calls it استئنافية, whereas the wa of 4:2:8 is CONJ/عاطفة; followed QAC. It could also be read as coordinating the prohibition with the preceding command (as QAC does for 4:2:8); the meaning is unchanged.
- **4:2:5:** Lemma written as the citation perfect تَبَدَّلَ per the lexeme entry; QAC lemma is the imperfect يَتَبَدَّلِ. Object ٱلْخَبِيثَ = what is taken, bi = what is given up. Tafsir differs on referents: Sa‘di (first) takes al-khabith as the orphan’s property taken unlawfully and al-tayyib as one’s own lawful property; Muyassar, Ibn Kathir’s reports and Sa‘di (second) describe swapping the orphan’s good item for the guardian’s inferior one. Urdu translation «اچھی چیز کو بری چیز سے نہ بدلو» kept neutral between the two.
- **4:2:11:** إِلَىٰٓ glossed as «کے ساتھ» (= مع, Sa‘di; Ibn Kathir: do not mix them). Followed the QAC note: the jar-majrur attaches to an implied ḥāl of أَمْوَٰلَهُمْ (e.g. مضمومةً إلى أموالكم). An alternative analysis treats تَأْكُلُوٓا۟ as implying تَضُمُّوا (taḍmīn), with the phrase attached to the verb.
- **4:2:13:** The ـهُ of إِنَّهُۥ is analysed as referring to the prohibited act (eating/substitution) understood from the verbs, not as ضمير الشأن, since كَانَ takes a hidden هُوَ as its ism with the same referent. QAC i‘rab for كَانَ says only فعل ماض; the ism/khabar statements follow standard i‘rab.
- **4:2:15:** QAC tags حُوبًۭا as a plain noun (M, INDEF, ACC) with no number tag; some lexicographers also list حُوب as a masdar of حَابَ (to sin). Taught as a noun meaning sin, per QAC.
- **4:3:28:** qacCorrection followed: QAC tags تَعُولُوا۟ jussive, but after أَلَّا (أَنْ + لَا) it is subjunctive (nūn dropped as the sign of naṣb); the ayah note also treats «ألا تعولوا» as an interpreted masdar. The generated i‘rab line (مجزوم) was not followed.
- **4:3:20:** QAC morphology tags the fāʾ of فَوَٰحِدَةً REM and its i‘rab line says استئنافية, but the ayah grammar note says الفاء رابطة لجواب الشرط with واحدة the object of an implied فانكحوا. Followed the ayah note (condition-answer), per the resumption rule.
- **4:3:22:** QAC i‘rab says only اسم موصول; the lesson treats مَا as coordinated with وَٰحِدَةً by أَوْ (محل نصب) with an elided resumptive object pronoun in مَلَكَتْ (مَلَكَتْهُ). Standard analysis, but not spelled out in the packet.
- **4:3:24:** QAC labels أَيْمَـٰنُكُمْ masculine plural; يَمِين "right hand" is generally treated as feminine in the lexica. The lesson states no gender for it and explains the feminine verb مَلَكَتْ by the broken-plural subject.
- **4:3:25:** Referent of ذَٰلِكَ: Sa‘di takes it as restriction to one wife or right-hand possessions; Muyassar extends it to the whole ruling (orphans, one to four, one or slaves). Both mentioned in dictionaryUrdu.
- **4:3:28:** Meaning follows Muyassar (الجور) and Sa‘di (تظلموا). The shared root entry ع و ل mentions a minority sense "having many dependents"; it is not in the provided tafsir, so it is mentioned only briefly in dictionaryUrdu and not in tafsirUrdu.
- **4:3:13:** مَثْنَىٰ / ثُلَـٰثَ / رُبَـٰعَ: ḥāl from مَا and diptote (وصفية + عدل) per the ayah note. number-words was not tagged because these distributive (معدول) numerals do not follow the counted-noun rules that rule describes. The و is explained as choice (two or three or four) following Muyassar and Sa‘di, not addition.
- **4:3:4:** QAC LEM تُقْسِطُ is an imperfect handle; lemma written as the perfect أَقْسَطَ per the lexeme entry.
- **4:3:23:** QAC LEM مَلَكَتْ is the 3FS form; lemma written as the citation form مَلَكَ per the lexeme entry.
- **4:3:6:** ٱلْيَتَـٰمَىٰ is glossed as orphan girls following Muyassar and Sa‘di (يتامى النساء); QAC lemma يَتِيم kept.

- **4:4:5:** Word-level QAC morphology and i‘rab label the prefixed fa resumptive. The ayah-level i‘rab explicitly coordinates the conditional sentence with the preceding command, so the lesson follows that analysis.
- **4:4:10:** QAC English calls the suffix an object pronoun. Its syntactic position is genitive under min, and the phrase qualifies the preceding indefinite noun through an understood modifier.

- **4:5:5:** The generic fork lemma is masculine. The occurrence and lesson lemma use the feminine singular relative, agreeing with a nonhuman plural. The understood resumptive ha is the first object of ja‘ala.
- **4:5:6:** The isolated English gloss sounds passive, but QAC and vocalisation show active perfect ja‘ala with Allah as the explicit subject.
