# Source audit: Surahs 105–109

Research date: 2026-10-23. Status: source inventory checked and AI-assisted Urdu editorial drafts authored with the user's approval. These lessons require scholarly review. This audit records source checks and editorial choices, not scholarly approval.

## Scope and source method

- Arabic words come from the Quran.com Content API `text_uthmani` field, excluding end-of-ayah markers. The API spelling and marks are preserved verbatim in the inventory below. The unnumbered introductory basmalah is not inserted into these surahs’ numbered word inventories.
- Each word links to the QAC morphology page actually retrieved. Root labels and finite-verb/participle distinctions were checked there. Morphology summaries below are compact technical facts rather than copied dictionary or translation prose.
- Each ayah links to the QAC grammar page actually retrieved; its own attribution is to the Quran Printing Complex. Empty verse notes are explicitly recorded rather than filled in and falsely attributed.
- QAC root indexing is not always a claim about historical etymology. Proper names/particles without a recorded root remain unassigned.
- The hosted abridged Ibn Kathir commentary was read for context. No third-party Urdu prose or complete dictionary/book text has been imported. Existing source attribution and license notices remain applicable.

## 105 — Al-Fil

Arabic inventory: <https://api.quran.com/api/v4/verses/by_chapter/105?words=true&word_fields=text_uthmani&per_page=50>

Commentary consulted: <https://quran.com/al-fil/1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir>

The commentary discusses the defeat of the army associated with the elephant and the protection of the sanctuary. A short lesson should stay with the ayahs’ stated sequence and avoid presenting every narrative detail as equally established.

## Editorial decisions and remaining review

- All five lessons contain original AI-assisted Urdu word glosses, morphology, syntax, dictionary summaries, root notes, verb paradigms, ayah translations, phrase explanations and short tafsir summaries. These are drafts for review, not quotations from a named Urdu scholar or a claim of approved religious instruction.
- The Arabic inventory contains 101 occurrences in 25 ayahs. The 24 finite-verb occurrences each have a paradigm. Every word is covered by at least one local rule and one ayah phrase; Arabic text and occurrence boundaries are checked against the saved source fixture.
- Visible segments concatenate exactly to each original Arabic word. Pedagogical segmenting can be finer than QAC's token grouping: 109:1:2 separates the written portions for يا, أي and ها, while the explanation identifies the vocative construction. 108:1:1 stays one visible segment because its fused spelling must not be presented as independently written pronouns. The omitted possessive yā in 109:6:4 is explained in prose and is not inserted into the Quranic word.
- QAC treats 106:3:1 as an imperfect verb made jussive by the imperative lām. Its word-type label remains فعلِ مضارع مجزوم, with the command explained separately. The Shaddah in 107:2:3 and 107:3:2 represents doubled Form I roots, while 107:6:3 is Form III. Participles are nouns, not finite verbs.
- The selected analysis of 108:3:3 is ضمير فصل. In 109:6:4 the noun remains syntactically nominative; its visible kasrah accompanies the elided possessive yā. These distinctions are written into the occurrence-specific explanations.
- Alternative syntax and interpretations remain visible where material: the attachment of the initial lām in Surah 106, the linking analysis of the fā in 108:2:1, lexical cautions for أبابيل and سجيل, and the range of ماعون. Scholarly review should check these choices, the newly authored Urdu phrasing, and explanatory generalizations before publication as reviewed teaching material.
